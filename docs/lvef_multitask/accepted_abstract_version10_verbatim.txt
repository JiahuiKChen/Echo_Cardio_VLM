Background: Echocardiography AI studies commonly report single-endpoint, single-modality performance, leaving uncertainty about whether multimodal methods scale across broader quantitative cardiac phenotypes in public datasets.

Methods: We conducted a retrospective study using MIMIC-IV-ECHO with a deterministic SCC-first pipeline and fixed subject-level splitting (one study per subject). The cohort included 4,530 eligible studies, with split allocation fixed at 3,171 train, 679 validation, and 680 test subjects. Multi-frame DICOM cine data were encoded with EchoPrime (encoder-only) into 512-dimensional embeddings and aggregated to study level. Structured measurements were exported and curated with unit diagnostics and leakage controls (669,378 rows; 186 raw measurement names). For the primary endpoint, we evaluated left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) on the linkage intersection cohort (eligibility + measurement linkage + embedding/keyframe linkage; n=2,833, test n=426). We compared three models: vision-only, structured-measurement-only (with LVEF leakage controls), and multimodal fusion. For expansion beyond LVEF, measurements were canonicalized into 178 grouped tasks, then filtered into a strict multitask panel of 29 numeric tasks with known preferred units and minimum support thresholds.

Results: In the held-out LVEF test set (n=426), the vision-only model achieved AUC 0.9581, R2 0.6127, MAE 5.6761 EF points; the structured-measurement-only model achieved AUC 0.9153, R2 0.4383, MAE 6.6842; and the multimodal fusion model achieved AUC 0.9618, R2 0.6615, MAE 5.3935. Structured-measurement curation excluded 4 explicit LVEF leakage features and 23 low-coverage features, retaining 51 predictors for the structured-measurement-only and fusion models. Relative to the vision-only model, multimodal fusion improved R2 by +0.0489 and reduced MAE by 0.2826 EF points. In 10,000 bootstrap resamples, AUC 95% confidence intervals were 0.9334-0.9784 for vision-only, 0.8734-0.9513 for structured-measurement-only, and 0.9402-0.9797 for multimodal fusion. In strict multitask evaluation (29 tasks), macro test mean R2/mean MAE-normalized-by-IQR were 0.3196/0.4953 for vision-only, 0.4385/0.4191 for structured-measurement-only, and 0.4886/0.3973 for fusion, indicating the strongest overall performance from multimodal fusion across heterogeneous structured measurements.

Conclusions: In a large public echocardiography cohort, multimodal fusion improved both LVEF prediction and broader multitask structured-measurement prediction versus unimodal baselines. This establishes a reproducible benchmark for perioperative and critical-care echocardiography AI development and supports next-phase conditional generation studies. Because this is a single public-source retrospective pipeline, external validation and prospective workflow evaluation remain necessary.
